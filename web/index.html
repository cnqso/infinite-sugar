About the work

What counts as
a good life?

A simulated fruit fly, given endless sugar.

Its neural wiring comes from a real fly. A simplified model of that network runs here, in your browser, and drives the body you see.

“Positive feeding signals” counts feeding signals while sugar is on. Whether any of this feels like anything is an open question.

A little further inside

139,255 neurons. 2,700,513 connections. Some movements use supplied patterns driven by neural activity. There is no learning or habituation.

Feeding signals are spikes in the proboscis and ingestion motor populations. The counter records them while sugar is on and holds when sugar is off. This includes background activity during sugar, not just spikes caused by it. It is not a measure of pleasure. A fresh page load starts a new simulation.

The sensory buttons stimulate selected neural populations. The terrarium is a setting, rather than a complete sensory world.

The neural view samples real FlyWire positions and connections. Blue marks sensory neurons, pink internal neurons, and yellow motor neurons and descending commands. Flashes follow simulated spikes, briefly extended for visibility; a connection lights when its source fires. Lines join representative positions and do not trace the shapes of neurons.
